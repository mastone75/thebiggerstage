01 The challenge

An identity-translation problem, not a language one

Japanese founder-CEOs who command respect at home routinely go invisible — or generic — the moment they operate in English abroad. Most speak workable to strong English; that isn't the gap.

The instincts that earn respect in Japan — modesty, indirection, letting the work speak — quietly undercut a founder in a US business culture that rewards narrative compression, personal ownership of a story, and the ability to make a stranger care in under three minutes.

02 Why now

Domestic growth in Japan is contracting by design

For founders chasing real scale, going global is shifting from optional ambition to a strategic response to structural contraction at home. International markets mean growing customer bases, larger talent pools, diversified revenue, and the reach to commercialize Japanese technology and brands.

03 The program

A performance intensive — not a classroom

The Global Breakthrough equips up to ten Japan-based founders or leaders to pitch, and to be remembered, on the global stage. Participants audition in, prepare over online pre-sessions, then spend 3 days in Tokyo crafting, performing, and filming their story in English, on stage and on camera. Each participant receives a produced media package built to travel — and a path to a New York debut.
