The method

The Revelation

Every founder who makes the operator-to-icon shift goes through the same internal transition first. The meaning behind the business, what it truly stands for, why it matters beyond the service, has to become accessible before it can become communicable. Most operators don’t know exactly what’s blocking them until someone asks the right questions.


The Revelation is a recorded conversation process designed to draw out what you haven’t been able to say. Not to install a message. To surface the true one. And put it on camera, where it becomes real.

Your story at scale

When the message is clear and on camera, it travels without you.

Most founders are the bottleneck of their own brand. Every new partner, hire, or market requires them to show up in person and explain, again, what the business is, why it matters, and why they're the one to lead it.

This is what separates an icon from an operator. An operator's impact is limited by their presence. An icon's message scales beyond it.

About Matt

He's been in a lot of rooms.

A law office. Federal agencies. The highest levels of leadership at the Pentagon. Multinational boardrooms. Hollywood studios. Silicon Valley startups. Leadership teams navigating change. The contexts changed. The work never did.

In every room, the same thing mattered most: the ability to listen deeply enough to draw out what someone couldn't quite say, and reflect it back in a way that changed everything. A case that settled. A pitch that landed. A team that finally understood why the work mattered. A leadership team turned around.

Growing up as a military upbringing that kept the family moving, and a career that never stopped, took Matt across seven states, three continents, and into rooms most people only read about. Always the new person. Always reading an unfamiliar room. He learned early that curiosity and genuine interest in people weren’t just social skills. They were how you survived. And they became the foundation of a career built entirely on helping people find and express what’s truest about themselves.

For ten years, he built a management consultancy on someone else's foundation, carrying another founder's meaning, speaking another founder's words. He got far. But something never fully landed. He knew that gap intimately. The personal cost of a presence that doesn't match what you've actually built.

That gap became The Bigger Stage. The operator-to-icon journey isn’t just a framework he teaches. It’s one he lived.
